The rule

  1. Every trading day, compute the Sensex's 200-day average: the average of its last 200 closing prices.
  2. Close above the average: stay invested.
  3. Close below the average: sell the next day and park the money in safe debt (6% a year assumed).
  4. When the close moves back above, buy back the next day.

Why it costs return

  • False alarms (whipsaws): the market dips below the line, you sell, and it recovers. You buy back higher.
  • Late by design: the rule sells after a fall has started and buys after a rise has started. It never catches the top or the bottom.
  • Time out of the market: 24% of the time the money sat in safe debt while stocks often rose.

Costs the backtest leaves out

  • Tax: in India, selling listed equity within 12 months is taxed as short-term capital gain (20% since July 2024). A rule with ~7 switches a year triggers it often.
  • Behaviour: selling into a falling market and buying back after a rally feels wrong. The rule only works if you follow every signal.
  • Dividends: the test uses the price index. Real index funds also collect dividends while invested.

Who this suits

  • Someone who would panic-sell in a 50% crash. A rule that halves the fall is worth more than a higher return they cannot hold through.
  • Someone close to a goal (retirement, a house) who cannot wait years for a recovery.
  • Not for someone who will skip signals, or who pays high tax on every switch.
